Five Words

Saturday
22 August 2026

Five words, each in a sentence with some weight. The line drawing is the main clause of that sentence.

Diagram: subject | verb | object. A backslash after the verb is a predicate adjective. Words hanging off the line are modifiers.

01

ephemeral

adj · /əˈfɛməɹəl/ · Greek ephḗmeros, “for a day” · 1560s

It does not last. A sunset, a trend, a mood. Not the same as rare.

The rainbow over the lake was ephemeral, gone before we reached the dock.

02

pragmatic

adj · /pɹæɡˈmætɪk/ · Greek prâgma, “a thing done” · via Latin

You pick what works over what is ideal. The decision, not the object.

She took a pragmatic approach and fixed the leak before anyone wasted a new coat of paint.

03

candid

adj · Latin candidus, “white, sincere” · 1620s

Honest in a plain, unpolished way. Not cruel. You skip the sugar.

His candid review named the broken parts of the plan instead of pretending the launch was fine.

04

mitigate

verb · /ˈmɪtɪɡeɪt/ · Latin mītis, “mild, soft” · via Middle English

Make it less bad, not make it gone. If you wipe it out, that is eliminate.

Extra practice can mitigate test anxiety, but it will not erase the feeling.

05

conspicuous

adj · /kənˈspɪkjuəs/ · Latin conspicere, “to look at” · 1540s

Easy to notice. The opposite of blending in.

The empty chair at dinner was conspicuous, and nobody wanted to mention it first.
