Ten Words

Meaning first. Origin sits quiet under the word. The line drawing is a Reed–Kellogg diagram of the example.

Diagram: subject | verb | object. A backslash after the verb is a predicate adjective. Words hanging off the line are modifiers.

01

ephemeral

adj · /əˈfɛməɹəl/ · Greek ephḗmeros, “for a day” · 1560s

It does not last. A sunset, a trend, a mood. Not the same as rare.

The rainbow was ephemeral.

02

pragmatic

adj · /pɹæɡˈmætɪk/ · Greek prâgma, “a thing done” · via Latin

You pick what works over what is ideal. The decision, not the object.

She took a pragmatic approach.

03

candid

adj · Latin candidus, “white, sincere” · 1620s

Honest in a plain, unpolished way. Not cruel. You skip the sugar.

His candid review named the problem.

04

mitigate

verb · /ˈmɪtɪɡeɪt/ · Latin mītis, “mild, soft” · via Middle English

Make it less bad, not make it gone. If you wipe it out, that is eliminate.

Extra practice can mitigate anxiety.

05

ubiquitous

adj · Latin ubique, “everywhere” · 1800

It seems to be everywhere you look.

Coffee shops are ubiquitous downtown.

06

terse

adj · Latin tersus, “wiped clean”

Short on purpose, almost clipped.

He gave a terse answer.

07

tacit

adj · Latin tacere, “to be silent” · via French

Understood without anyone saying it.

They reached a tacit agreement.

08

arduous

adj · Latin arduus, “steep, hard to reach”

Hard in a grinding way, not a single sharp shock.

The rewrite was arduous.

09

conspicuous

adj · /kənˈspɪkjuəs/ · Latin conspicere, “to look at” · 1540s

Easy to notice. The opposite of blending in.

The empty chair was conspicuous.

10

tentative

adj · Latin tentare, “to try” · 1580s

Not firm yet. A first try you can still take back.

We made a tentative plan.

Check

You put a bright orange cone in the hallway so nobody misses the wet floor. Is the cone conspicuous, or ubiquitous?
