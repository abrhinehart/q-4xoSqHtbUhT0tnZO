Five Words

Monday
24 August 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. The drawing is only the main clause.

Diagram: subject | verb | object. A backslash after the verb is a predicate adjective. Words hanging off the line are modifiers.

01

austere

adj · Greek austēros, “harsh, dry” · via Latin · 1300s

Severe and spare. No extra comfort, no extra ornament.

The room of the cabin was austere: a cot, a stove, and a window that leaked cold air.

02

copious

adj · Latin copia, “abundance” · 1300s

A lot of it. Not a taste. A pile.

Copious notes filled the desk by Friday, enough to write the report twice over.

03

tenuous

adj · Latin tenuis, “thin, stretched” · 1590s

Thin. A link that might snap. A claim with little under it.

The case against him was tenuous, resting on one late email and a rumor from last spring.

04

lucid

adj · Latin lucidus, from lux, “light” · 1590s

Clear to the mind, as if a light is on it. Easy to follow.

Her memo was lucid after the rewrite: one claim, three facts, and no fog.

05

prudent

adj · Latin providere, “to look ahead” · 1300s

Careful because you can see what is coming. Not timid. Not reckless.

He sold the shares before the news broke, a prudent move that saved the quarter.
