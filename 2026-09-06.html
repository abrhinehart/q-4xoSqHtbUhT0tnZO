Five Words

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

esoteric

/ˌɛsəˈtɛɹɪk/ · ess-uh-TAIR-ik

adj · Greek esōterikós “inner circle” · 1650s

Meant for a small initiated circle — hard for outsiders to follow without the shared background.

The briefing stayed esoteric enough that only the three people who had lived inside the codebase nodded, while everyone else waited for a translation into plain English.

02

indelible

/ɪnˈdɛləbl/ · in-DEL-uh-bul

adj · Latin indelebilis “imperishable” · 1520s

Impossible to erase or forget — a mark, stain, or memory that stays.

That one clumsy email left an indelible mark on the hire’s first week, and months later people still quoted the typo whenever his name came up in staffing talks.

03

placate

/ˈpleɪkeɪt/ · PLAY-kate

v · Latin placātus ← placāre “to calm” · 1670s

To calm someone’s anger or resentment — to soothe and win back peace.

She tried to placate the furious vendor with a partial payment and a rewritten schedule, knowing a sharper tone would only lock the dispute in place for another week.

04

furtive

/ˈfɝtɪv/ · FUR-tiv

adj · Latin furtīvus “stolen, secret” via French · 16c

Done by stealth — secretive, as if hoping not to be noticed.

His furtive glance at the clock during the toast told the room he was already halfway out the door before the applause had finished.

05

heinous

/ˈheɪnəs/ · HAY-nus

adj · Old French hainos “hateful” ← haine “hatred” · late 14c

Shockingly wicked or evil — so bad it feels beyond ordinary wrongdoing.

The prosecutor called the fraud heinous not for the dollar amount alone, but because it preyed on retirees who had trusted the firm with their last savings.
