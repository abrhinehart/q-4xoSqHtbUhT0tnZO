Five Words

4 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

sycophant

/ˈsɪkəfænt/ · SIK-uh-fant

noun · Greek sykophántēs via Latin ← sûkon “fig” + phaínō “to show” · 1530s

A person who praises the powerful too much, and without sincerity, to get something from them.

The room filled with sycophants the moment the founder walked in, each one praising a slide they had mocked an hour earlier in the hallway.

02

obsequious

/əbˈsiːkwi.əs/ · ub-SEE-kwee-us

adj · Latin obsequiōsus ← obsequium “compliance” ← ob + sequī “to follow” · late 15c

Trying too hard to please someone important — fawning, unduly compliant.

His obsequious laugh trailed every joke the partner told, even the ones that died in the middle of the room and left everyone else staring at their notes.

03

churlish

/ˈt͡ʃɝːlɪʃ/ · CHUR-lish

adj · Old English cierlisc ← churl + -ish · rude sense late 14c

Rude, surly, ungracious — deliberately sour when courtesy was due.

It felt churlish to refuse the invitation after they had rearranged the whole evening for him, but he sent a one-line no and went home early anyway.

04

puerile

/ˈpjʊɹəl/ · PYOO-ruhl

adj · Latin puerīlis ← puer “boy” · 1660s

Childish in a way that looks silly on an adult — immature, lacking seriousness.

The board called the meme campaign puerile, not because humor was banned, but because it treated a safety recall like a joke contest.

05

taciturn

/ˈtæs.ɪˌtɝn/ · TAS-ih-turn

adj · Latin taciturnus ← tacitus “silent” · 1650s

Habitually silent or reserved in speech — saying little, often in a way that reads as unfriendly.

She was taciturn in meetings, saving her words for the memo that landed after everyone else had already talked themselves into a corner.
