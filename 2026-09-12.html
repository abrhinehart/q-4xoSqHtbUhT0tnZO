Five Words

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

surreptitious

/səˌɹɛpˈtɪʃəs/ · suh-rep-TISH-us

adj · Latin surrēptīcius “furtive, clandestine”

Done on the sly — stealthy or covert, especially a move you hope nobody notices.

He made a surreptitious swap of the seating chart so the two people who keep derailing the meeting would not sit next to each other again.

02

extant

/ˈɛk.stənt/ · EK-stuhnt

adj · Latin extāns “standing out, existing” · 1545

Still around — not lost, destroyed, or extinct; still in existence.

Only three of the original board decks are still extant, and the rest live only as screenshots in old Slack threads.

03

concomitant

/kənˈkɑmətənt/ · kun-KOM-uh-tunt

adj · Latin concomitāns via Middle French · early 17c.

Riding along with something else — accompanying or concurrent, not the main show.

The raise came with a concomitant jump in after-hours Slack, which nobody had written into the offer letter.

04

derisive

/dɪˈraɪsɪv/ · dih-RYE-siv

adj · Latin dērīdeō “to deride” + -ive

Mocking — a tone, laugh, or remark that ridicules rather than engages.

The first question from the panel was already derisive, aimed less at the idea than at making the founder look small.

05

propitious

/pɹəˈpɪʃəs/ · pruh-PISH-us

adj · Latin propitius “favorable” via Anglo-Norman/Old French

Favorable — a moment, omen, or condition that works in your favor.

With rates easing and the competitor distracted, it was a propitious week to soft-launch the quieter pricing plan.
