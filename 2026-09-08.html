Five Words

Tuesday
8 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

sagacious

/səˈɡeɪʃəs/ · suh-GAY-shuhs

adj · Latin sagāx “keenly perceptive” · c. 1600

Keenly discerning — shrewd judgment and farsightedness, not just book smarts.

The sagacious counsel waited until the second round before naming a price, because the buyer’s eagerness was still too loud to trust.

02

querulous

/ˈkwɛrələs/ · KWER-uh-luhs

adj · Latin querulus “full of complaints” · c. 1400

Habitually complaining — fretful or whiny in tone, as if every detail is a grievance.

His querulous notes about the coffee temperature and the chair height turned a five-minute standup into a slow tour of tiny grievances.

03

obfuscate

/ˈɑbfəskeɪt/ · AHB-fuh-skayt

v · Latin obfuscare “to darken” · 1530s

To deliberately make something confusing — darken the truth so it is harder to see.

The filing seemed designed to obfuscate the fee schedule: three annexes, two footnotes, and still no plain number for what the customer would pay.

04

panache

/pəˈnæʃ/ · puh-NASH

n · French panache “plume of feathers” · 1550s (flair sense later)

Flamboyant style or swagger — energetic flair that makes an ordinary move look bold.

She closed the pitch with panache, skipping the apologetic Q&A and ending on one crisp demo that left the room quiet for a beat.

05

bombastic

/bɑmˈbæstɪk/ · bom-BAS-tik

adj · bombast “padding” + -ic · 1704

Pompous and inflated in speech — high-sounding words with little real substance.

The bombastic press release promised a “paradigm shift,” then admitted in paragraph four that the product was still a waitlist and a logo.
