Five Words

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

rancorous

/ˈræŋkərəs/ · RANG-kər-uhs

adj · rancor + -ous · 1580s

Full of lasting bitterness — resentful and unforgiving rather than a brief flare of anger.

The merger talks turned rancorous when both sides started dredging up old board fights instead of pricing the deal.

02

profligate

/ˈprɑːflɪɡət/ · PROF-lih-gət

adj · Latin prōflīgātus “ruined, corrupt” · extravagant by 1779

Recklessly wasteful with money or resources — extravagant to the point of ruin.

His profligate travel budget burned through the quarter’s marketing fund on first-class flights and dinners no client ever attended.

03

supercilious

/ˌsu.pɚˈsɪ.li.əs/ · soo-per-SIL-ee-uhs

adj · Latin superciliōsus “haughty” · 1520s

Arrogantly superior — showing haughty contempt as if everyone else is beneath you.

She answered the intern with a supercilious smile that said the question was too basic to deserve a real reply.

04

phlegmatic

/flɛɡˈmætɪk/ · fleg-MAT-ik

adj · Greek phlégma via Latin · mid-14c.

Hard to excite — calm, steady, and unemotional even when others flare up.

While the room panicked over the outage, his phlegmatic update listed three next steps and a time for the next check-in.

05

irascible

/ɪˈræs.ə.bəl/ · ih-RAS-uh-bəl

adj · Latin īrāscī “be angry” · late 14c.

Easily provoked to anger — quick to snap when things go wrong.

The irascible editor rejected three drafts before lunch and sent the fourth back with a single red “No.”
