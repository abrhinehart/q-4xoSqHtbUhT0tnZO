Five Words

Friday
18 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

urbane

/ɝˈbeɪn/ · ur-BAYN

adj · Latin urbānus via French urbain · refined manners sense 1620s

Having refined manners — courteous, polite, and suavely polished.

His urbane reply to the angry vendor cooled the room without conceding a single line of the contract.

02

gauche

/ɡoʊʃ/ · GOHSH

adj · French gauche “left, awkward” · 1751

Awkward or lacking in social graces — apt to make gaffes.

The gauche toast at the wedding dinner named the wrong bride’s mother and then tried to joke it off.

03

obstreperous

/əbˈstɹɛpəɹəs/ · uhb-STREP-uh-ruhs

adj · Latin obstreperus “clamorous” · c. 1600

Noisily defiant — stubbornly resistant to authority or control.

An obstreperous shareholder shouted over the chair until security walked him out mid-sentence.

04

pusillanimous

/ˌpjuːsɪlˈænɪməs/ · pyoo-suh-LAN-uh-muhs

adj · Late Latin pusillanimis “faint-hearted” · early 15c

Showing contemptible timidity — cowardly in a small-souled way.

It was a pusillanimous silence: everyone saw the error, and no one risked saying it in the meeting.

05

disingenuous

/ˌdɪsɪnˈdʒɛnjuəs/ · dis-in-JEN-yoo-uhs

adj · dis- + ingenuous · 1650s

Not frank or candid — insincere, often posing as naïve.

Her disingenuous “surprise” at the leaked memo fooled no one who had sat in the planning call.
