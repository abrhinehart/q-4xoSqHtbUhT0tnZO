Five Words

Monday
14 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

vituperative

/vəˈtuːpɚətɪv/ · vuh-TOO-per-uh-tiv

adj · Latin vituperō “to blame, censure” · late 15c.

Marked by harsh, ranting verbal abuse — bitterly scolding rather than a single rebuke.

The columnist’s vituperative takedown spent two thousand words calling the mayor corrupt, lazy, and unfit, without once proposing a fix.

02

diffident

/ˈdɪfɪdənt/ · DIF-ih-dənt

adj · Latin diffīdere “to mistrust” · mid-15c.

Lacking self-confidence — timid or modest in manner, hesitant to assert yourself.

She knew the answer but stayed diffident in the meeting, waiting until someone else floated the same idea before she nodded.

03

exuberant

/ɪɡˈzuːbəɹənt/ · ig-ZOO-bər-ənt

adj · Latin exūberāns “abundant” · mid-15c.

Very high-spirited and enthusiastic — cheerful energy that spills over.

His exuberant welcome — hugs, jokes, and a tray of coffee — made the new hire feel like the office had been waiting for her all week.

04

protean

/ˈproʊ.ti.ən/ · PROH-tee-ən

adj · Proteus + -an · from 1590s

Exceedingly variable — readily taking different shapes, roles, or forms.

Her protean career jumped from courtroom to startup to podcast host, and somehow each version still sounded like the same person.

05

ignominious

/ɪɡ.nəˈmɪ.ni.əs/ · ig-nuh-MIN-ee-uhs

adj · Latin ignōminiōsus “disgraceful” · early 15c.

Marked by disgrace or public shame — humiliating in a way that sticks.

After the product demo crashed live on stage, the team’s retreat to the green room felt like an ignominious end to six months of work.
