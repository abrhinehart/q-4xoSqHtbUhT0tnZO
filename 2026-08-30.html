Five Words

Sunday
30 August 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

erudite

/ˈɛɹ.jə.daɪt/

adj · Latin ērudītus, “learned, brought out of the rough” · early 15c

Learned, especially from books. Scholarship that actually shows.

After the Q&A, her erudite aside on the 1970s case law made the junior associates realize they had briefed the wrong era.

02

capricious

/kəˈpɹɪʃəs/

adj · French capricieux, from Italian capriccio · 1590s

Ruled by whim. The plan changes because a mood did.

The vendor’s capricious shipping dates left the kitchen waiting on a stove that was “tomorrow” three times in a week.

03

magnanimous

/mæɡˈnæn.ɪ.məs/

adj · Latin magnanimus, “great-souled” · 1580s

Generous when you could be small. You let the slight go.

He was magnanimous in the debrief, giving the intern credit for the catch instead of letting the miss sit on her name.

04

desultory

/ˈdɛs.əlˌtɔɹ.i/

adj · Latin dēsultōrius, “leaping from horse to horse” · 1580s

Jumping from thing to thing with no through-line. Effort without a plan.

Their desultory search through the inbox — a subject line here, a half-read thread there — never found the approval they needed by Friday.

05

feckless

/ˈfɛkləs/

adj · Scots feck, “effect, vigor,” + ‑less · 1590s

Ineffective and irresponsible. No grip, no follow-through.

The feckless handover left three open tickets and a login nobody had written down, so Monday started in the dark.
