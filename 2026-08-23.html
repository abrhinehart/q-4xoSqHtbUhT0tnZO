Five Words

Sunday
23 August 2026

Five new ones. The sentence carries the word. The line drawing is only the main clause.

Diagram: subject | verb | object. A backslash after the verb is a predicate adjective. Words hanging off the line are modifiers.

01

laconic

adj · Greek Lakōn, Laconia (Sparta) · 1580s

Short on purpose. The Spartans were famous for this: much meaning, almost no words.

His laconic reply was a single word, and it ended the argument at the table.

02

ostensible

adj · Latin ostendere, “to show” · 1730s

The reason held out as true. It may be the real one. It may not.

The ostensible reason for the meeting was a budget review, though everyone knew it was a firing.

03

prosaic

adj · Latin prosa, “prose” · 1650s

Ordinary, like prose instead of a poem. Fine for a Tuesday. Thin as a story.

Monday felt prosaic after the weekend: same commute, same desk, same lunch from the fridge.

04

salient

adj · Latin salire, “to leap” · 1560s

The point that jumps forward. Not merely easy to see. The one that matters.

The salient point in the report was the missing money, not the polished charts on page one.

05

equivocate

verb · Latin aequus + vocare, “equal voice” · 1400s

Talk so the words can mean two things, usually to avoid a straight answer.

When the board asked if the numbers were clean, he began to equivocate instead of answering.
