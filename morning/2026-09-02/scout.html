Fed / Barr

September hike on the table

Fed Governor Michael Barr put a September hike on the table Tuesday. At the Second-Chance Lending Forum he said inflation has been above target for over five years, and if it is “not moderating sufficiently” the FOMC should “act decisively to raise rates”; if data show a path to 2%, he can wait. That lands after Chair Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole hawkishness.

Same afternoon President Trump said the U.S. was striking Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz; WTI jumped above $90 (three-month high) and CME FedWatch moved to roughly 70% odds of a hike at the Sept. 15–16 meeting (up from ~65% Monday and under 40% a week earlier). Friday’s August jobs print and Sept. 11 CPI are the last major labor and inflation reads before FOMC. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent argued it would be a mistake to hike against a supply-shock oil spike; markets are not pricing that restraint.

Clarity Act

Cloture ripens Sept 15

Senate Majority Leader John Thune’s cloture motion on H.R. 3633 (Digital Asset Market Clarity Act) ripens Tuesday, Sept. 15 at 2:15 p.m. EDT. That is cloture on the motion to proceed, not final passage: 60 votes needed to open floor consideration. Republicans cannot clear it alone; roughly seven Democratic or independent votes are still the gap.

Success only starts debate (then a second cloture and possible House conference if the Senate amends). Failure stalls the procedural path without killing the bill. Calendar pressure rises as midterms pull floor time.
