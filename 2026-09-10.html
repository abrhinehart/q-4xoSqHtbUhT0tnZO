Five Words

Thursday
10 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

cogent

/ˈkoʊdʒənt/ · KOH-juhnt

adj · Latin cōgere “to drive together, compel” via French · mid-17c.

Clear and convincing — an argument that persuades because the reasoning holds, not because it shouts.

She made a cogent case for delaying the launch: three failing checks, one missing rollback plan, and a support queue already at capacity.

02

flagrant

/ˈfleɪɡrənt/ · FLAY-gruhnt

adj · Latin flagrāns “blazing” via French · late 15c.

Shockingly obvious as a wrong — done so openly that nobody can pretend not to see it.

Copying the client deck into a personal folder was a flagrant breach of the NDA, not a gray-area mix-up about shared drives.

03

quixotic

/kwɪkˈsɑtɪk/ · kwik-SAH-tik

adj · English from Don Quixote + -ic · 1791

Nobly impractical — chasing an ideal with more romance than realism.

His quixotic plan to rebuild the whole legacy stack in one sprint ignored the three teams still shipping from the old code.

04

anomalous

/əˈnɑmələs/ · uh-NOM-uh-luhs

adj · Greek anṓmalos “uneven” via Late Latin · 1640s

Deviating from the pattern — irregular enough that it deserves a second look.

The anomalous spike in refunds on a quiet Tuesday made finance pull the weekend’s payment logs before anyone blamed marketing.

05

redolent

/ˈɹɛdələnt/ · RED-uh-luhnt

adj · Latin redoleō “to emit a scent” via Old French · c. 1400

Strongly suggestive — of a smell, or figuratively of a mood, place, or earlier time.

The empty office after the move was redolent of late nights and dry-erase fights, even with the furniture already gone.
