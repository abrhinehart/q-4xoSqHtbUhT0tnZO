Five Words

Thursday
17 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

petulant

/ˈpɛtʃələnt/ · PECH-uh-luhnt

adj · Latin petulāns via Middle French · 1590s

Childishly irritable — peevish and easily annoyed over small things.

His petulant reply to a one-line schedule change turned a routine standup into a twenty-minute argument about respect.

02

stoic

/ˈstoʊ.ɪk/ · STOH-ik

adj · Greek Stōïkós via Latin stōicus · impassive by 1590s

Enduring hardship or emotion without complaint — outwardly calm and uncomplaining.

She stayed stoic through the overnight outage, logging each failure without raising her voice once.

03

pensive

/ˈpɛnsɪv/ · PEN-siv

adj · Old French pensif “thoughtful” · late 14c

Deep in serious or melancholic thought — reflective rather than merely quiet.

After the farewell toast he grew pensive at the window, turning his glass and saying nothing for a long minute.

04

cantankerous

/kænˈtæŋkəɹəs/ · kan-TANG-kuh-ruhs

adj · contentious + rancorous (dial.) · 1772

Ill-tempered and quarrelsome — cranky in a way that picks fights.

The cantankerous neighbor disputed every shared fence post until the city inspector finally measured the line himself.

05

vehement

/ˈviː.hə.mənt/ · VEE-huh-muhnt

adj · Latin vehemēns “impetuous, ardent” · early 15c

Forceful and intense — showing strong feeling with heat rather than mild disagreement.

Her vehement defense of the junior engineer stopped the blame spiral before it could harden into a written reprimand.
