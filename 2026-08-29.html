Five Words

Saturday
29 August 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

circumspect

/ˈsɝ.kəm.spɛkt/

adj · Latin circumspectus, “looking around” · early 15c

Cautious on purpose. You check the edges before you step.

He stayed circumspect in the meeting, naming the risk without blaming anyone until the numbers were confirmed.

02

vacillate

/ˈvæ.sə.leɪt/

verb · Latin vacillare, “sway, hesitate” · 1590s

To swing between choices. Not thinking — dithering.

She vacillated between accepting the offer and asking for one more week until the deadline forced her hand.

03

fastidious

/fæsˈtɪdi.əs/

adj · Latin fastīdiōsus, “squeamish, exacting” · mid-15c

Hard to please about small things. Precision that can tip into fussiness.

His fastidious review of the deck caught a mislabeled axis that would have wrecked the client’s first glance.

04

innocuous

/ɪˈnɑkjuəs/

adj · Latin innocuus, “harmless” · 16th c

Harmless and unprovoking. Nothing in it that could start a fight.

He opened with an innocuous question about the weather so the room could settle before the hard ask.

05

succinct

/səkˈsɪŋkt/

adj · Latin succīnctus, “girded, concise” · late Middle English

Brief and packed. No spare words, but the point lands whole.

Her succinct reply named the decision, the owner, and the Friday deadline in a single sentence.
