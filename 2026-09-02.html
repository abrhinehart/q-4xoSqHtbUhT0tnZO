Five Words

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

ephemeral

/əˈfɛ.mə.ɹəl/ · uh-FEM-uh-ruhl

adj · New Latin ← Greek ephḗmeros, “lasting a day” · 1560s

Lasting only a short time. Here today, gone before you can lean on it.

The startup’s early buzz proved ephemeral: six months after the launch party, the waitlist was cold and the investors had already moved on.

02

pragmatic

/pɹæɡˈmætɪk/ · prag-MAT-ik

adj · French pragmatique ← Greek pragmatikós, “fit for business” · 1850s

Practical — choosing what works in the real situation, not what looks best on paper.

Faced with a deadline and a half-finished redesign, she made the pragmatic call to ship the working core and defer the flourish.

03

alacrity

/əˈlæk.rə.ti/ · uh-LAK-ri-tee

noun · Latin alacritās ← alacer, “brisk” · mid-15c

Eager, brisk willingness. Not grudging help — moving before anyone has to ask twice.

He accepted the overnight shift with surprising alacrity, already packing a thermos before the schedule was even posted.

04

pedantic

/pəˈdæn.tɪk/ · puh-DAN-tik

adj · pedant + -ic ← Italian pedante · c. 1600

Overly concerned with minor rules or showing off book-learning — correct in a tiresome way.

The reviewer’s pedantic note on a single Oxford comma drowned out any useful comment on the argument itself.

05

truculent

/ˈtɹʌk.jə.lənt/ · TRUK-yuh-luhnt

adj · Latin truculentus ← trux, “fierce” · 1530s

Eager to fight or argue; aggressively defiant, not merely stubborn.

The truculent board member interrupted every proposal with a personal attack, until the chair finally cut his mic.
