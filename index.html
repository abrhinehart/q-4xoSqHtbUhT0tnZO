Five Words

Wednesday
26 August 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. The drawing is only the main clause.

Diagram: subject | verb | object. A backslash after the verb is a predicate adjective. Words hanging off the line are modifiers.

01

perfunctory

adj · Latin perfungi, “to get through” · 1580s

Done to be done with it. The letter of the duty, none of the spirit.

His perfunctory nod at the door told her he had already checked out of the meeting.

02

reticent

adj · Latin tacere, “to be silent” · 1822

Held back in speech. Not mute. Unwilling to say the extra thing.

She was reticent about the offer, and the silence did more work than a speech would have.

03

fortuitous

adj · Latin fors, “chance” · 1650s

By accident, not by plan. It does not mean lucky, even when the accident helps.

The fortuitous delay at the gate put him on the later flight, which was the one that actually left.

04

insipid

adj · Latin in- + sapidus, “tasty” · 1610s

No flavor. Of talk or food: dull, nothing to taste.

The speech was insipid: three slogans, no numbers, and a joke that had already died twice.

05

obviate

verb · Latin obviam, “in the way” · 1590s

Clear it out of the way in advance, so the problem never arrives.

A written agenda would obviate the usual twenty minutes of asking what we were doing there.
