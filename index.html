Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

castigate

/ˈkæs.tɪ.ɡeɪt/ · KASS-tih-gate

verb · Latin castīgō “to reprove” · early 17c.

To reprimand or criticize someone severely — formal, public, and sharp.

The board chair did not merely disagree; she castigated the CEO for burying the churn numbers three slides deep.

02

cajole

/kəˈd͡ʒoʊl/ · kuh-JOHL

verb · French cajoler

To coax someone into something they are reluctant to do, usually with flattery or soft promises.

He tried to cajole the engineer into shipping Friday by praising her last release and dangling Friday-off as bait.

03

apocryphal

/əˈpɑkɹɪfəl/ · uh-POK-rih-fuhl

adj · Apocrypha + -al · from 1590s

Of doubtful authenticity — a story that circulates as fact but will not hold up under scrutiny.

The founder origin story about coding the first version overnight in a garage is almost certainly apocryphal.

04

vapid

/ˈvæp.ɪd/ · VAP-id

adj · Latin vapidus “flat, vapid”

Offering nothing stimulating — dull, flat, empty of challenge or spark.

After three vapid keynotes that restated the press release, the hallway conversations were sharper than the stage.

05

stentorian

/stɛnˈtɔɹ.i.ən/ · sten-TOR-ee-uhn

adj · Stentor + -ian (Iliad herald)

Extremely loud and powerful — usually of a voice built to carry across a crowd.

His stentorian call from the back of the room stopped the side chatter before the moderator even reached for the mic.
