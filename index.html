Five Words

Friday
18 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

sanguine

/ˈsæŋ.ɡwɪn/ · SANG-gwin

adj · Latin sanguineus via Old French · optimistic sense c. 1500

Anticipating the best — optimistic, confidently hopeful rather than gloomy.

Even after the third delay, she stayed sanguine about the launch, arguing that the bugs were finally shrinking rather than multiplying.

02

truculent

/ˈtɹʌk.jə.lənt/ · TRUK-yuh-luhnt

adj · Latin truculentus “fierce, savage” · 1530s

Eager or quick to argue or fight — aggressively defiant rather than merely grumpy.

His truculent pushback in the all-hands left the new manager choosing every word as if the room might explode.

03

taciturn

/ˈtæs.ɪˌtɝn/ · TAS-ih-turn

adj · Latin taciturnus via French taciturne · 1650s

Habitually silent — temperamentally untalkative and disinclined to speak.

The taciturn analyst sat through the entire postmortem without a word, then emailed three surgical notes that ended the debate.

04

loquacious

/loʊˈkweɪʃəs/ · loh-KWAY-shuhs

adj · Latin loquāx “talkative” + -ous · 1660s

Talkative and chatty — given to continual talking.

A loquacious seatmate turned the short flight into a two-hour autobiography that left no silence unfilled.

05

magnanimous

/mæɡˈnæn.ɪ.məs/ · mag-NAN-ih-muhs

adj · Latin magnanimus “great-souled” · 1580s

Noble and generous in spirit — elevated above petty resentment.

She was magnanimous after winning the contract, publicly crediting the rival team for the insight that improved the bid.
