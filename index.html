Five Words

Saturday
5 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

facetious

/fəˈsiːʃəs/ · fuh-SEE-shus

adj · French facétieux ← Latin facētia “jest” · 1590s

Treating a serious matter with inappropriate humor — flippant when gravity was due.

His facetious crack about the layoff slides landed in a dead silence, and the room spent the next minute deciding whether to laugh or report him.

02

gregarious

/ɡɹɪˈɡɛɚ.i.əs/ · grih-GAIR-ee-us

adj · Latin gregārius “of a flock” ← grex “flock” · 1688

Enjoying company and crowds — sociable by nature, happiest in a group.

She was gregarious enough to turn a two-table dinner into a standing hallway conversation that somehow still ended with three new collaborators and a shared calendar invite.

03

voracious

/vəˈɹeɪ.ʃəs/ · vuh-RAY-shus

adj · Latin vorāx “devouring” (via voracity) · 1630s

Having a huge appetite — for food, or for books, news, or work.

His voracious reading left stacks on every surface, and by Friday he had already finished the briefings the rest of the team was still skimming.

04

brusque

/bɹʌsk/ · BRUSK

adj · French brusque ← Italian brusco · 1650s

Abrupt and curt in manner — short with people when a softer tone was expected.

The manager’s brusque “handle it” cut the question off mid-sentence, and the intern left without knowing whether the deadline had moved or simply been ignored.

05

hapless

/ˈhæpləs/ · HAP-liss

adj · hap “luck” + -less · c. 1400

Unlucky; unfortunate — dogged by bad breaks rather than by malice.

The hapless courier arrived with the wrong box, a dead phone, and a parking ticket, then watched the client sign for someone else’s shipment and walk away.
