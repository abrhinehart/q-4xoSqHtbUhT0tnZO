Five Words

7 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

mendacious

/mɛnˈdeɪʃəs/ · men-DAY-shuhs

adj · Latin mendāx “lying” · 1610s

Given to lying — dishonest, or of a claim that is false on purpose.

The investor walked after one mendacious slide about “signed contracts,” because a single invented customer was enough to poison every number that followed.

02

loquacious

/loʊˈkweɪʃəs/ · loh-KWAY-shuhs

adj · Latin loquāx “talkative” · 1660s

Given to continual talking — chatty, rarely short of words.

The loquacious seatmate narrated every layover delay for two hours, until the cabin lights dimmed and even he finally ran out of airport lore.

03

dogmatic

/dɔɡˈmætɪk/ · dog-MAT-ik

adj · Greek dogmatikos “of doctrine” · 1680s

Asserting opinions as absolute truth — pushing them without room for evidence or dissent.

His dogmatic claim that remote work “never scales” shut down the discussion before anyone could share the retention numbers that contradicted him.

04

umbrage

/ˈʌmbrɪdʒ/ · UM-brij

n · Latin umbra “shade” via Old French · early 15c (offense sense 1610s)

Offense or resentment at a slight — the feeling of being overshadowed or insulted.

She took umbrage at being left off the invite list, reading the silence as a deliberate snub rather than a calendar glitch.

05

inchoate

/ɪnˈkoʊət/ · in-KOH-ut

adj · Latin incohātus “begun, unfinished” · 1534

Just begun and not yet fully formed — still rudimentary or incomplete.

The pitch was still inchoate: a handful of sketches and a hunch about pricing, with no prototype sturdy enough to demo for the board.
