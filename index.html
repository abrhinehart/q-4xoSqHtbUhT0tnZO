Five Words

Thursday
3 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

ambivalent

/æmˈbɪv.ə.lənt/ · am-BIV-uh-luhnt

adj · back-formation from ambivalence ← German Ambivalenz ← Latin ambi- + valeō · 1916

Torn between two feelings or ideas at once — not undecided from lack of care, but pulled both ways.

She felt ambivalent about the promotion: the title and pay were real, but so was the cost to every evening she still had with her kids.

02

egregious

/ɪˈɡɹiː.d͡ʒəs/ · ih-GREE-juhs

adj · Latin ēgregius ← ex grege, “out of the flock” · 1530s

Outrageously bad — so conspicuous a failure that it stands out from ordinary mistakes.

The report’s egregious omission of the safety data forced the board to retract it before the vote, not after the damage.

03

iconoclast

/aɪˈkɑn.əˌklæst/ · eye-KAH-nuh-klast

noun · French iconoclaste ← Medieval Latin ← Greek eikonoklástēs, “image breaker” · 1590s

Someone who attacks cherished beliefs or institutions — a breaker of sacred images, then of sacred ideas.

He made his name as an iconoclast in the field, publishing papers that dismantled assumptions everyone else treated as settled law.

04

precipitous

/pɹɪˈsɪpɪtəs/ · pri-SIP-ih-tuhs

adj · obsolete French précipiteux ← Vulgar Latin *praecipitōsus · 1640s

Steep like a cliff — or, of a change, dangerously sudden and headlong.

After the leak, the stock’s precipitous drop wiped out a year’s gains before lunch, and the trading desk went quiet.

05

temerity

/təˈmɛɹ.ə.ti/ · tuh-MER-uh-tee

noun · Old French temerité ← Latin temeritās, “rashness” · early 15c

Reckless boldness — the nerve to try something others would call foolish, or the cheek to do it anyway.

It took real temerity for the junior analyst to contradict the CEO in open meeting, and the room held its breath until she finished.
