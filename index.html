Five Words

Monday
31 August 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

obdurate

/ˈɑb.dʒə.ɹɪt/

adj · Latin obdūrātus, “hardened” · mid-15c

Stubbornly unmoved. Soft appeals bounce off; the position does not yield.

The board stayed obdurate on the hiring freeze even after three departments showed they could not cover the open shifts.

02

specious

/ˈspiː.ʃəs/

adj · Latin speciōsus, “good-looking” · late 14c

Sounds right and looks polished, but the reasoning is false once you press it.

His specious case for skipping the audit hung on one cherry-picked chart that fell apart when finance pulled the raw ledger.

03

mollify

/ˈmɑl.ɪ.faɪ/

verb · Latin mollis, “soft” (via mollificō) · late 14c

To soften anger or worry. Not erase the problem — cool the heat around it.

She mollified the vendor with an honest apology and a revised ship date before the dispute hardened into a chargeback.

04

ubiquitous

/juˈbɪk.wɪ.təs/

adj · Latin ubīque, “everywhere” (via ubiquity) · 1830s

Seeming to be everywhere at once. So common it stops feeling like a choice.

QR menus had become so ubiquitous that the one paper card on the table felt like a deliberate courtesy, not an oversight.

05

panacea

/ˌpæn.əˈsiː.ə/

noun · Greek panakeia, “all-healing” (via Latin) · mid-16c

A cure-all. One fix sold as if it could settle every problem at once.

Treating a new tool as a panacea for missed deadlines only hid the real issue: nobody owned the handoff between design and shipping.
