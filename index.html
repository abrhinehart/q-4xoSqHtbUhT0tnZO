Five Words

Friday
28 August 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

trenchant

/ˈtɹɛnʃənt/

adj · Old French trenchant, “cutting” · late 14c

Cuttingly clear. Not loud. It names the point and leaves the padding behind.

Her trenchant note on the budget cut through three pages of hedging and named the one number that actually mattered.

02

proclivity

/pɹoʊˈklɪvɪti/

noun · Latin proclivitas, “tendency” · 1590s

A natural leaning toward something. Habit waiting for a chance.

He had a proclivity for rewriting emails at midnight, then sending the first draft anyway before dawn.

03

deleterious

/ˌdɛ.ləˈtɪɹ.i.əs/

adj · Greek dēlētērios, “noxious” · 1640s

Harmful, often quietly. The damage shows up after the habit feels normal.

Skipping the walk every weekday had a deleterious effect on his sleep long before the scale caught up.

04

nascent

/ˈneɪ.sənt/

adj · Latin nascens, “being born” · 1620s

Just coming into being. Early enough that the shape can still change.

The nascent side project still lived in a notebook, but two clients had already asked when it would ship.

05

acerbic

/əˈsərbɪk/

adj · Latin acerbus, “sour, bitter” · 17th c

Sharp and tart in tone. Bite more than burn.

Her acerbic reply to the vendor ended the call in under a minute, and nobody asked for a follow-up.
