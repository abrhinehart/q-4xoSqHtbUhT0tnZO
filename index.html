Five Words

Tuesday
1 September 2026

Five new ones. The sentence has to carry the word. Tap Hear to say it out loud.

01

recalcitrant

/ɹɪˈkæl.sɪ.tɹənt/ · ri-KAL-si-truhnt

adj · French récalcitrant ← Latin recalcitrāre, “kick back” · 1820s

Stubbornly defiant of authority. Not just unmoved — actively kicking against the order.

The recalcitrant contractor ignored three written change orders and kept pouring concrete to the original, wrong elevations.

02

perspicacious

/ˌpɜːɹ.spɪˈkeɪ.ʃəs/ · per-spi-KAY-shəs

adj · Latin perspicāx, “sharp-sighted” · 1630s

Keen insight. Sees through the surface of a problem before others notice the crack.

Her perspicacious read of the customer emails spotted the churn pattern weeks before the dashboard finally turned red.

03

assiduous

/əˈsɪd͡ʒu.əs/ · uh-SIJ-oo-əs

adj · Latin assiduus ← ad + sedēre, “sit by” · 1530s

Steady, careful effort. Sitting with the work until it is actually finished.

Assiduous note-taking through every vendor call meant she could rebuild the timeline when legal asked who had promised what.

04

paucity

/ˈpɔs.ɪ.ti/ · PAW-si-tee

noun · Latin paucitās ← paucus, “few” · late 14c

A real shortage — few in number or thin in amount. Not “less than ideal”; barely enough to work with.

The paucity of overnight staff left one nurse covering a floor that the schedule still listed as fully manned.

05

solicitous

/səˈlɪsɪtəs/ · suh-LIS-ih-təs

adj · Latin sollicitus, “anxious, careful” · 1560s

Showing care or concern, often eagerly. Checking in because the person (or outcome) actually matters.

He was solicitous about her recovery in a way that was not performative: quiet texts, a ride to the follow-up, no speeches.
